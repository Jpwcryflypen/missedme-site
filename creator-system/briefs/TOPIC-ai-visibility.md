## The one-line premise
Six in ten buyers now start their home search by asking an AI. Nine in ten agents never appear in the answer. The portals are rebuilding their toll booth inside AI, spammers are gaming the answers with planted Reddit comments, and the legit playbook is sitting there unused.

## Act 1 — The stat (cold open, fear)
- **Safe phrasing:** "According to a 2026 industry benchmark covered by NAR and HousingWire, roughly 6 in 10 buyer searches now start in an AI tool. And about 9 in 10 agents never show up in the answers. The top 1% of agents are capturing 47% of all AI mentions."
- Attribution honesty: the 61.3%/91% numbers come from ONE vendor benchmark (FlyDragon, an AI-SEO company). NAR and HousingWire covered it, which is the citable part. Say "industry benchmark," never "NAR study."
- Independent backup stats (safe to use): consumer use of ChatGPT for local business recommendations jumped 6% → 45% in one year (BrightLocal). ChatGPT recommends only ~1.2% of local businesses at all.
- ⚠ NEVER repeat: "AI leads close 70% in 30 days" or "80% of transactions will involve AI by Q4" — vendor hype, uncorroborated.

## Act 2 — The villain (Zillow, anger)
- **The verified ammunition:**
  - Premier Agent is a >$1B/year business; placement is bought ($1,500–5,000+/mo), not performance-verified. Flex referral fees run 15–40% of the agent's commission (typically 35%, raised to 40% in six markets).
  - **The Wharton study (the killer beat):** a controlled experiment by Wharton emeritus professor Jerry Wind found **99.7% of consumers clicking "Contact Agent" on Zillow believed they'd reach the listing agent — they're actually routed to agents paying for the lead.** His words: Zillow's interface "systematically deceives consumers." (SSRN 6140990; covered by Fox Business.)
  - Zillow launched the ONLY real estate app inside ChatGPT at launch (Oct 2025), and says every lead "flows through Zillow's existing channels" — i.e., the same paid pipes. Zillow AI Mode (March 2026) can "connect me with an agent."
- **Precision rules (so comments can't wreck you):**
  - Say: "the connections flow through the same paid-lead system." Do NOT say "Zillow's AI recommends whoever pays" — they haven't published how AI Mode ranks agents; that's an inference.
  - The Zillow v. Compass/MRED case (hearings July 1–2, THIS WEEK) is about listing-data control, not AI agent placement. Use as context: "the portals are literally in federal court this week fighting over who controls the data that feeds these AIs." Not: "Zillow's AI is on trial."
- **The hopeful turn (thesis of the video):** even the vendor data shows Zillow's share of agent discovery FALLING (41% → 34% YoY) as open AI tools grow. The open-web AI surfaces are the one place a solo agent can out-position a billion-dollar portal without paying anyone.

## Act 3 — The wild west (the "wait, WHAT" segment)
Sequence it as an escalation:
1. **Glue on pizza** (2024): Google's AI told people to put glue on pizza — traced to an 11-year-old Reddit joke comment. Why? Google pays Reddit ~$60M/yr for its data; OpenAI has a deal too. Reddit is a top-3 cited source on nearly every AI surface.
2. **The industry** (June 2026, 404 Media exposé): marketing firms now openly sell "AEO" — paid Reddit posts that get cited by ChatGPT within 24 hours. Aged accounts, paying real Redditors, seeding fake "Is X legit?" questions and answering them. A 830k-member subreddit (r/biohackers) locked down after promotional flooding.
3. **The paper (this is the thread John remembered):** Cornell Tech researchers published the "WARP" attack (June 2026): **a 13-word snippet added to one well-placed Reddit comment reliably steers AI answers.** They tested it on local recommendations — one planted comment made AIs recommend a restaurant. Direct analogy on camera: "One comment in your city's subreddit could put a fake 'best realtor' in every AI answer in your market."
4. **The comedown (stay the trustworthy guy):** Reddit is deploying AI to catch this and suing scrapers; the FTC's fake-review rule (2024) squarely covers astroturfed testimonials; and getting outed as an astroturfer in your own market subreddit is reputational suicide. **Frame: "This works. That's exactly why it's dangerous, why regulators are circling, and why you build the legit version instead."** We report the trick; we never teach it.

## Act 4 — The legit playbook (the payoff + the offer)
Ranked by evidence (each maps to what AIs actually cite):
1. **Reviews — volume, recency, and SPREAD across 4+ platforms** (Google, Zillow, Realtor.com, Yelp, Facebook). Works on every AI surface; ChatGPT leans on review aggregators; Perplexity literally ingests Yelp's API. *(This is the MissedMe cameo — one line, no pitch.)*
2. **Complete Google Business Profile + consistent directory profiles** (Zillow, Realtor.com, HomeLight). Those directory pages ARE what AI quotes for "best agent in [city]" — you must exist on the pages AI already reads. GBP + reviews ≈ half of local ranking weight (Whitespark 2026).
3. **One genuinely specific local page on your own domain** — "[Neighborhood] market, this month: median price, days on market, 3 things buyers should know" with real statistics and cited sources. The Princeton GEO study (the only academic anchor in this field): adding citations, quotes, and statistics boosts AI visibility 30–40%. And 58% of ChatGPT's local sources are business websites — your own site matters more than people think.
4. **Get named on third-party lists** — local news, "best of [city]" roundups, podcasts. There's an authority threshold below which nothing else counts.
5. **Schema markup + sameAs links** (RealEstateAgent/Person schema tying site ↔ GBP ↔ Zillow ↔ LinkedIn). One-afternoon job; structured data drives over half of citations across engines (Yext).
6. **The white-hat Reddit move:** answer questions in your city subreddit under your real name, with disclosure. Same mechanism the spammers abuse, done honestly.
- Timeline honesty: first citations in 4–8 weeks (Perplexity fastest, 1–2 weeks), durable visibility 6–12 months. Early movers compound.

## The on-camera demo (do this live, it carries the video)
Ask the SAME question — "Who's the best buyer's agent in [Phoenix] for first-time buyers?" — across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude. Expect four different name sets (only ~11% of cited domains overlap between engines — say this stat when the results differ). Then click the citations on screen: they're Zillow/HomeLight "best of" pages, review aggregates, and one or two agent blogs. That's the whole lesson made visible: *AI doesn't have an opinion about agents. It has sources. Be in the sources.*

## Per-surface cheat sheet (for the video's "how it actually works" beat)
- **ChatGPT:** Bing index; trusts directories + review sites ("what the internet agrees on"). Listing queries get routed into the Zillow app; agent-choice queries still draw on the open web.
- **Gemini / Google AI Mode:** Google index + Google Business Profile. GBP is the lever.
- **Perplexity:** own crawler + Yelp API + heaviest Reddit citer. Fastest to index new content.
- **Claude:** runs on Brave Search — ~87% of its citations match Brave's top results.
- No AI has a "realtor ranking algorithm." They synthesize whatever ranked pages, directories, reviews, and threads exist. Agents with zero third-party footprint literally cannot appear.

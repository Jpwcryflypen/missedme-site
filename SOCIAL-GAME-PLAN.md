## TL;DR — The Whole Plan in Ten Lines

1. **Niche (first 90 days):** AI for the property world — real estate agents first, with home-services pros and RE photographers as adjacent segments. Not "AI for everyone" (saturated). Your ADHD story is the voice, not the niche (yet).

3. **Platforms:** YouTube is home base (1 long-form + 3 Shorts/week). LinkedIn is career insurance (3–5 posts/week). TikTok/Reels are free reposts.
4. **Workflow:** One batch day per week. Package first (title + thumbnail before you record), script on teleprompter, record everything in one session, AI edits, you review.
5. **Editing pipeline, phase 1 (buy, don't build):** Descript ($24/mo) auto-edits the long-form; Vizard ($19/mo) cuts and schedules the Shorts. Publish video #1 within 7 days.
6. **Editing pipeline, phase 2 (month 2+):** Custom Claude-agent pipeline (this exists as open source now — `video-use`, Descript API) once the buy-it stack has proven you'll actually publish.
7. **Cultural relevance:** Automated weekly trend brief + 30 minutes of your time. Never fake fluency — the self-aware "I'm 40 and I read about this meme in a newsletter" position is funnier and safer.
8. **Your three agents:** Research assistant (trend brief + topic research), script writer (teleprompter-formatted, hook-first, cringe-filtered), video editor (Descript now, custom later). Specs below.
9. **ADHD operating system:** Everything runs in two-week sprints with a published artifact at the end of each. Nothing gets built that you don't use within 14 days. (The Etsy lesson, encoded as a rule.)
10. **Realistic math:** ~250 days is the *average* to 1,000 YouTube subs. That's fine — your first $1k doesn't come from the algorithm, it comes from one workshop. Content builds the asset; services bridge the money.

## 1. The Niche Decision (I Changed My Recommendation — Read This)

My initial default was "everyday people catching up on AI" with professional niches as segments. **The research says that's backwards:**

- Generic "AI for everyone" is owned by incumbents (Matt Wolfe, The AI Advantage, Futurepedia, Zain Khan) and actively saturated. Platforms now algorithmically suppress generic AI content with no original insight.
- **Profession-specific AI is the documented white space.** "AI for real estate agents": agent AI adoption jumped from 11% (2023) to 35%, Udemy is stuffed with courses but YouTube-native educator competition is thin, and the advertiser base (mortgage, CRM, brokerage platforms) supports reported CPMs of $22–35 vs $2–4 for generic AI content (that CPM figure is single-source — treat as directional, but the competitive-density gap is real).
- All three platforms now *mechanically* reward topical tightness: YouTube clusters your first ~10–15 videos to learn who to show you to; LinkedIn's new LLM-based algorithm (360Brew) scores your posts against your profile headline — scattered topics = suppressed reach.
- The verified creator case studies (Nate B. Jones, Grace Leung, Nate Herk) all share it: one tight audience, stated in the positioning, held for months.

**Recommendation: "AI for the property world."** Real estate agents are the tip of the spear; RE photographers and home-services/trades pros (your MissedMe people) are natural adjacent rings of the same audience. This is your unfair advantage — you're not a tech guy cosplaying; you've built for trades (MissedMe), you shoot, you inspect roofs, you know what these people's Tuesdays look like.

**Where the other stuff goes:**
- **Everyday-people AI content** → your Shorts testing ground. Shorts run on a separate algorithm; use them to test broad hooks cheaply. What hits can justify broadening in month 4+.
- **ADHD + AI** → your *voice and origin story*, threaded through everything ("I built this because my brain deletes projects after two weeks"). It becomes its own content pillar only after you've proven the system on yourself — which is exactly the conclusion you already reached.
- **Photographers** → a recurring segment inside the property wedge (RE photography is the bridge).

*Override switch: if after 30 days the property content feels like a costume and the broad/ADHD stuff is what you actually make, switch. A niche you won't show up for is worth nothing. But give the wedge 30 days first.*

## 2. The Monetization Path (Decided Up Front, Like You Asked)

### The honest numbers first

- Audience-first content typically takes **18–36 months to $1k/month, and about half of creators never sustain it** (NeoReach 2025 survey n=3,000+; Gumroad median active creator earns ~$72/mo). Every "monetized in 90 days" story that survives scrutiny had pre-existing distribution.
- BUT: the most profitable revenue stream for expert creators is **consulting/coaching/workshops, and it comes FIRST, not last** (The Tilt benchmark research — 5 months to first dollar, 13 months to full-time is the median for content entrepreneurs). One AI training sold to a brokerage at normal rates ($1.5k–5k) clears a month's $1k target in an afternoon.
- And: **you don't need a big audience.** Kajabi's platform data: the average six-figure creator has 1,000–10,000 followers, ~4,000 emails, and only ~309 paying customers. Small and specific beats big and vague.

### The ladder (in order)

| When | Rung | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | **Email list + free Skool** | Skool Hobby plan ($9/mo). Lead magnet: "The 10 AI moves for agents" PDF/mini-course. Every video points here. Email is the asset no algorithm can take away. |
| Weeks 2–6 | **Live workshops (first real money)** | "AI for Your Office" — 90-min live training pitched to brokerages/teams, $500–1,500 flat (Phoenix market first, Zoom after). Your videos are the sales deck. This is the ADHD-compatible fast win: sell it before you over-build it. |
| Month 2–3 | **Paid community tier** | $37–47/mo inside Skool (the verified sweet spot: AI Advantage converts at $37, Nate Herk's AIS+ at $99 with support calls). Launch only once the free community shows you what people actually ask for. Include a monthly live call. |
| Month 4–6 | **Recorded course / paid challenge** | $97–297, built from the workshop you've now delivered live several times. Never build the course before the live version has sold. |
| Month 6+ | **Sponsorships** | Nano-tier YouTube ($50–500/video) and newsletter placements become real once you have 1k+ engaged subs in a niche with advertiser budgets (real estate tech loves this audience). |

### Platform notes (from the research)

- **Skool**: Hobby $9/mo with a 10% transaction fee → switch to Pro ($99/mo, ~2.9%) around $1,300/mo community revenue. Known warts: billing/cancellation complaints on Trustpilot, no email marketing built in (hence your own list), and its discovery feed skews get-rich-quick — **external traffic (your YouTube/LinkedIn) is mandatory, which is fine because that's the whole plan.** Alternatives if Skool grates: Circle ($89/mo, better courses/branding) or Whop (no monthly fee, ~3%/sale, but hustle-culture adjacency and fund-hold complaints).
- Rule from the case studies: **bundlers earn ~4.5x more than single-product creators.** The ladder above ends with 4–5 streams (workshops + community + course + sponsorships + MissedMe cross-pollination). That's the design, not an accident.

## 3. Platforms & Cadence (6–10 hrs/week Version)

| Platform | Cadence | What & why |
|---|---|---|
| **YouTube long-form** | 1/week, 4–8 min | The trust-and-revenue engine. YouTube judges each video on its own merits — no channel-history penalty, 18% of top search results come from sub-1k-sub channels. Consistency beats volume; the real bar is 40+ videos over 9–12 months. |
| **YouTube Shorts** | 3/week | Discovery + hook testing. Separate algorithm from long-form (a flopped Short cannot hurt your videos). 15–30s sweet spot, keyword-rich titles. |
| **LinkedIn** | 3–5 posts/week | Career insurance + where agents/brokers actually are. Format ranking from the 1.3M-post Algorithm Insights 2026 report: document/PDF carousels #1 (~6.6% engagement), native vertical video #2, text+image, then text. **Update your headline to the niche first — the algorithm scores posts against it.** Never 2 posts same day; reply to every comment in the first hour. No external links in the post body (~19% reach cut — links go in comments... which are also now suppressed; put the CTA as "comment KEYWORD and I'll DM it," which still works and is the dominant lead-gen mechanic). |
| **TikTok + Reels** | 3–5/week (reposts) | Near-zero extra cost once Shorts exist. Strip watermarks (TikTok watermark = Reels recommendation death). TikTok's 2026 algorithm rewards 3–5 min high-retention videos — your long-form can go there natively too. |
| **X** | Skip for now | Wrong audience for the wedge. Revisit if you go broad-AI later. |

**Realistic 90-day expectations (so the two-week brain doesn't rage-quit):** 100–300 YouTube subs is *normal and on-track*; 500–1,000 is a great outcome. Average time to 1k subs is ~254 days. Your first few hundred followers come from outside the algorithm — Phoenix real estate Facebook groups, agent subreddits, your own network, LinkedIn comments on big RE accounts. The 90-day scoreboard is: videos published, emails collected, workshops sold. Not subs.

## 4. The Weekly Machine (One Batch Day + Scraps)

**The atom is one long-form video per week. Everything else is derived from it.**

### Batch Day (~3.5–4 hrs, same day every week — protect it like a flight)

1. **Package first (45 min).** Per Paddy Galloway's rule: if you can't write a clickable title + thumbnail concept, it's not a video idea. Write title + thumbnail sketch for 2 videos BEFORE anything else. Study outliers in adjacent niches (videos that massively overperformed their channel's baseline) for packaging patterns.
2. **Script (45 min, agent-assisted).** The script agent (below) drafts from your outline; you punch it up in your own voice. 130–150 words/min, hook in first 5 seconds (result-first for tutorials: show the outcome, then the promise, then stakes — all inside 30 seconds). No "hey guys" intro. ~600–900 words for a 5-min video; 45–80 words for a Short.
3. **Record (60–90 min).** One take culture: long-form + 3 Short-specific verticals in one session. Teleprompter setup below. Flubs stay in the take — the AI cuts them.
4. **Send to edit + write LinkedIn (45 min).** Footage goes into the pipeline. While it processes, draft the week's LinkedIn posts: 1 PDF carousel distilled from the video, 1–2 text posts (a story or contrarian take), 1 native vertical clip.

### Rest of week (~2–3 hrs total)

- **Review the edit** (30–45 min): watch the AI cut, request changes, approve, schedule.
- **Daily 10–15 min engagement window**: LinkedIn golden-hour replies + 3–5 thoughtful comments on big real-estate/AI accounts (borrowed distribution — this is how new accounts actually grow).
- **30-min trend brief review** (Sunday or Monday — see Section 6).

## 5. The Editing Pipeline (Your "Record → AI → Review" Dream, in Two Phases)

### Phase 1 — Weeks 1–8: Buy it. Publish ugly. (~$45–55/mo)

The rule: **you must publish video #1 within 7 days of starting.** No custom pipeline gets built until the boring stack has shipped 6+ videos, because the failure mode isn't bad editing — it's building an editor instead of publishing (the Etsy lesson).

| Tool | Cost | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Descript Creator** | $24/mo | The workhorse. Drop in raw footage → Underlord removes filler words/silence/bad takes, cleans audio (Studio Sound), adds animated captions. **Underlord Custom Instructions = you write your editing style once as standing orders** ("cut all pauses over 0.5s, punch-in zoom on emphasis, captions style X") and it applies every time. Closest shipping thing to "AI that edits like me." Known warts: buggy on long projects, credits burn fast. |
| **Vizard Creator** | $19/mo | Long video in → captioned vertical Shorts out, + built-in scheduler for 6 social accounts (YouTube/TikTok/IG/LinkedIn), + API included on the cheapest paid tier (matters for Phase 2). Expect to trim clip boundaries — every clipper's AI misses ~60–80% of the time; you pick the 2–3 good ones. |
| **Meme bin** (free) | 1 hr to set up, 10 min/week to feed | A folder of 30–50 evergreen memes/GIFs/reaction clips, organized by emotion ("disaster," "mind blown," "suspicious," "money"). Memes get *manually* dropped at punch moments during your review pass — 10 minutes, and it's the step no tool automates well anyway (researched extensively: nothing auto-inserts curated pop-culture clips; Fireship places every meme by hand too — the writing is the moat, not the edit). |

**Copyright reality check** (so you can be relaxed about it): short meme inserts in long-form commentary are ubiquitous and effectively safe — worst common case is a Content ID claim that diverts that video's ad revenue, not a strike. **Shorts are different: a claimed clip gets the Short blocked outright.** So: memes and movie clips live in long-form; Shorts use your face, text, zooms, and sounds from the platform's own library. GIPHY GIFs are technically not licensed for commercial use but enforcement is essentially nonexistent; keep clips under ~3 seconds, cropped/overlaid, and you'll rarely even get claimed.

### Phase 2 — Month 2+: Build your editor agent (the fun part, earned)

The custom pipeline you described — record, hand off to an agent, review, request changes — **exists now as open source**, and I can build your version with you in this repo when you're ready:

- **`browser-use/video-use`** (15k stars, active): drop raw footage in a folder, talk to Claude Code, get back a finished cut — word-level transcript via ElevenLabs Scribe, filler/dead-space cuts at word boundaries, 30ms audio fades, burned captions, overlay generation. This is the skeleton.
- **Descript API** (open beta, May 2026, free with your subscription): drive Underlord programmatically — your Phase 1 custom instructions become an automated step.
- **HyperFrames / Remotion**: agent-generated motion graphics, captions, and branded overlays (agents write HTML/React far better than any timeline format).
- Your style lives as a versioned config: cut rules, caption style, zoom triggers, sound-effect keywords, meme-bin index the agent can pull from (it suggests placements; you approve — comic timing stays human).

Budget one two-week sprint for v1 of this, *after* the publishing habit exists. It's also a content goldmine: "I built an AI that edits my videos" is exactly the teach-people-to-do-what-I-do content that sells the community.

### Teleprompter (bridging to the INMO GO3)

- **Now:** script window tucked directly under the webcam (Camo or Speakflow, free) + Descript's eye-contact fix in post — corroborated as natural-looking when the gaze offset is small. If you want hardware: Elgato Prompter, $280, the standard.
- **INMO GO3 (arriving next month):** legit — shipped to Kickstarter backers late June 2026, binocular display, dedicated teleprompter mode with claimed AI pace-sync (unverified independently yet). Honest caveat from long-term smart-glasses users: **reading eyes are still visible in tight close-ups.** Fixes: frame wider/further back (longer focal length), use bullet-point anchors instead of verbatim script, or run a Descript eye-contact pass. For standing/walking/B-roll pieces they'll be great — and "I have a teleprompter on my face" is itself a video.
- **Script formatting for the glasses:** narrow column, short lines, ≥1.5 spacing, bolded anchor words — the script agent outputs this format automatically (spec below).

## 6. The Cultural Relevance System (Funny Without Living Online)

**Posture first, tools second:** Gen Z is in a "post-cringe" phase — faked fluency is the only fatal error. The winning position for you is self-aware: own being the guy who learns memes from a newsletter. Fireship's humor isn't "current memes," it's *in-jokes that flatter his audience's expertise* + deadpan delivery + absurd visual cutaways. Your version: jokes agents and contractors feel seen by ("the lockbox code is 1234 again"), AI-industry absurdity ("the clanker deleted prod"), and evergreen-absurdist memes over week-old TikTok audio (which ages badly on YouTube's long tail anyway).

**The automated weekly brief (your Research Assistant agent, part 1):** a scheduled job (n8n or a Claude routine — buildable in sprint 2) that compiles every Sunday night:

1. Newsletter digests: **Garbage Day** (internet culture), **After School** (Gen Z), **Geekout** (platform mechanic changes), ICYMI
2. Know Your Meme trending — critically, **each meme's AGE and lifecycle stage** (the thing that saves you from using a dead one; anything >8 weeks old goes on the auto-generated "do not use" graveyard list)
3. TikTok Creative Center trending sounds/hashtags + YouTube Shorts Trends page
4. Top posts from r/OutOfTheLoop, r/ChatGPT, r/singularity (the actual meme source for AI content)

Output: one page — 5 usable memes/sounds with one-line explanations and a safe-to-use flag, 3 format notes, 2 platform changes, the graveyard.

**Your 30 minutes:** read the brief (15 min), pick at most 1–2 trend hooks where the trend is a *container* for an AI-in-real-estate joke (never a straight recreation), watch 3–5 examples of the chosen trend so the cadence is right (15 min — the only mandatory scrolling in this entire plan).

**The millennial-cringe filter:** LLM-drafted humor defaults to millennial cadence — it's literally a named 2026 problem. The script agent's final pass strips: millennial pause phrasing, "adulting"-era vocabulary, 😂 as a reaction, Office/Friends references, and 2024 brainrot used unironically (skibidi/rizz/gyatt are throwback-only now).

## 7. Your Three Agents (Specs)

1. **Research Assistant** — two jobs: (a) the Sunday trend brief above; (b) per-video topic research — given next week's video topic, returns the 5 things worth saying, the contrarian angle, what the top 3 existing videos on it missed, and 3 title/thumbnail options scored against outlier patterns.
2. **Script/Annotation Writer** — takes your voice-memo ramble or bullet outline → teleprompter-ready script: hook-first structure, 130–150 wpm pacing, narrow-column INMO-formatted output with bolded anchors, [MEME: disaster] and [ZOOM] annotations inline for the editor, plus the Shorts scripts (45–80 words each) and the LinkedIn carousel outline derived from the same material. Final pass: cringe filter. **Tangent handling for ADHD:** the script includes labeled "tangent slots" — planned 15-second riff points — so going off-script has a place to live and a place to end.
3. **Video Editor** — Phase 1: Descript Underlord with your custom instructions + Vizard for Shorts. Phase 2: the custom pipeline in Section 5, which consumes the script agent's inline annotations so meme placement and zooms arrive pre-suggested in the draft cut.

All three start as Claude projects/routines in week 1 (an afternoon to set up) and get automated incrementally. None of them may consume a batch day.

## 8. The ADHD Operating System (The Actual Foundation)

Everything above is packaged to survive your two-week window:

- **Two-week sprints, each ending in a published artifact.** Not "progress" — a thing that is live and visible. The dopamine loop is the infrastructure.
- **The 14-day rule (the Etsy lesson):** nothing gets built unless you personally use it within 14 days. The trend brief passes (you read it weekly). The editor agent passes (it edits your videos). A course before a sold workshop fails. A second channel fails.
- **Speed beats quality for the first 10 videos.** They will be mediocre; that is the tuition, and YouTube doesn't punish you for it (each video is judged fresh — old flops don't drag new videos down).
- **The streak metric is "batch days completed," not subscribers.** One number, binary, weekly. Everything downstream follows from it.
- **Pre-commitment:** the workshop rung works because selling a training to a real office creates an external deadline your brain can't dismiss.
- **This is also the product.** "AI as an ADHD prosthetic — I built a system that gets me from idea to published in one day" is the most differentiated content you'll own, once it's true. Prove it on yourself first (your own conclusion; the research backs it: Tiimo won App of the Year, ADDitude/CHADD now publish AI-for-ADHD content — demand is real and the creator lane is not crowded).

### Sprint map

- **Sprint 1 (weeks 1–2): EXIST.** Channel + Skool + email list live. Buy-it stack configured. Meme bin v1. Record and publish videos #1–2 + 4 Shorts + 6 LinkedIn posts. LinkedIn headline updated to the niche.
- **Sprint 2 (weeks 3–4): SYSTEMATIZE.** Videos #3–4. Trend-brief automation live. Script agent tuned on your voice. First workshop pitch emailed to 10 Phoenix brokerages.
- **Sprint 3 (weeks 5–6): SELL.** Videos #5–6. Deliver workshop #1. Lead magnet live, plugged in every video.
- **Sprint 4 (weeks 7–8): BUILD.** Videos #7–8. Editor-agent v1 (now earned). Document the build as content.
- **Sprints 5–6 (weeks 9–12): DECIDE.** Videos #9–12. Read the data: which pillar overperformed, what does the free community ask for → shape of the paid tier. INMO GO3 integration video. Month-4 plan.
